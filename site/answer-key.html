Answer key

The numbers on this page come from the project's data file, so they always match the site. Differences are in percentage points.

Part A. Predict

  1. Test H1: …. The difference was ….
  2. Test H3: …. The difference was ….
  3. Test H5: …. The difference was ….
  4. Test H7: …. The share of outdated answers rose by ….
  5. Test H8: …. The share of outdated answers fell by only ….

Part B. Read the charts

  1. … of ….
  2. The difference is …. The range goes from … to ….
  3. Yes. The true difference could be a little below zero, zero, or a little above zero. So the study cannot say that the library helped. The plan also required at least +….
  4. For H3, the difference is …, and the range goes from … to …. The measured difference is above the +… that the plan required, and the whole range is above zero. For H1, the range includes zero.

Part C. Old law

  1. With nothing to read: …. With only the old decision: ….
  2. These search methods rank texts by how similar they are to the question. An old decision and a new decision about the same issue use similar words. The search does not check whether a later decision overruled a case.
  3. Good answers: check whether a later decision overruled the case; look at the date; use a tool that lists overruled cases (a citator); ask a lawyer.

Part D. Think

  1. No. “Not supported” means that this experiment did not show the effect clearly enough. A larger model, a better library or more questions might give a different answer.
  2. If researchers choose their tests after they see the results, they can pick the tests that happen to look good. Fixed rules prevent that.
  3. Many answers are good. Examples: rerun the scripts that compute the results; read a sample of the AI-written questions; check whether the AI's reasons for wrong answers are right.
