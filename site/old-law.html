The old-law trap

Courts sometimes overrule their own earlier decisions. After that, the old rule is no longer “good law”. A lawyer must check this. A search tool has no sense of time.

An example. In South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), the Supreme Court overruled two earlier decisions about sales tax on sales from other states. A person who reads only the earlier decisions learns a rule that courts no longer follow.

Overruling is a legal event. It does not say which side was morally right.

The US government publishes a table of Supreme Court decisions that the Court later overruled. From that table, AI models wrote 227 questions. Two of them later left the test, so the tests use 225. Each question has one answer with the current rule and one answer with the outdated rule.

An AI model wrote these questions, and a second AI model checked them. No person has checked them yet. The project treats the rule of the overruling decision as the current rule. A later law could also change a rule, and the questions do not check for that.
