A research project that you can check

Does a law library help a small AI model answer old bar exam questions?

Lawyers look things up all the time. This project gave small AI models some old multiple-choice questions from the US bar exam, with and without legal texts to read. Its plan fixed 11 tests and their rules for success before the main runs. This site shows what happened.

  • Not legal advice
  • Not bar exam scores
  • AI models did most of the work
  • No person has checked the code or the results yet

What we found

Some of the 11 tests came out supported, and the rest did not. Differences are in percentage points.

With the best library that the project could build, a legal encyclopedia, the model did not score higher than with nothing to read.

…

With the passage that a law student had marked as the right one, the model scored higher than with that encyclopedia.

…

When the model read only a court decision that was later overruled, it chose the outdated rule far more often.

Test H9 · see the tests

… of …

In this many questions, search ranked the overruled decision above the decision that overruled it.

So the right text helps, and old law is a trap. Two attempts to fix the trap did not help by a clear amount (tests H8 and H10). The tests do not show how often the search failed to find the right passage.

What this project cannot show

  • These are not bar exam scores. The questions are old exam questions released from 1972 to 1998. A score here says nothing about whether a model, or a person, could practise law.
  • Small models only. The tests used two small models on one laptop. Larger models may behave very differently.
  • “Not supported” does not mean “proven false”. It means that this experiment did not show the effect clearly enough.
  • Some questions are AI-written. An AI model wrote the questions about overruled decisions, and no person has checked them yet.
  • Nothing here is legal advice. The author is not a licensed US attorney.

Who made this, and who checked it
