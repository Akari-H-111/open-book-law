# Preregistration

Version 3, 2026-11-03. This project covers US law.

Version 1 of this file covered only Taiwan law, and version 2 held both. On 2026-11-03 the author moved Taiwan to a separate repository, `open-book-law-tw`, which holds that plan and its history. Nothing in Part I changed in that move, and the git history of this file records when each rule was fixed.

## 1. Questions

Imagine a candidate who starts the US bar exam from zero. Since July 2026, parts of the NextGen exam have been tested "with the provision of legal resources", so it also matters what the candidate can look up.

1. Do published baselines for legal retrieval reproduce on a fanless MacBook Air?
2. What library helps a small model most: doctrinal summaries, rules, or case law?
3. How much of the remaining error comes from retrieval, from reading, and from the law itself?
4. Does a retriever return law that is no longer good, and does the model follow it?
5. Does a retriever return the right rule from the wrong state?

This is a research benchmark. It gives no legal advice. Scores on old exam questions do not measure legal ability, and they are not bar exam scores.

## 3. Systems

### 3.1 Retrievers

| ID | Method | Fixed settings |
|---|---|---|
| `bm25` | BM25, written by hand, over lowercase word tokens | k1 = 0.9, b = 0.4 |
| `e5-small`, `e5-base`, `e5-large` | `intfloat/e5-*-v2`, cosine, with `query:` and `passage:` prefixes | Model defaults |
| `hybrid` | Reciprocal rank fusion of `bm25` and the best dense model on validation | k = 60, not tuned |
| `rollout` | Any retriever with the query extended by a structured reasoning rollout (the paper's Table 13 prompt) | Generated by the local reader, not by GPT-3.5 |
| `ft-small` | `e5-small` fine-tuned by us (section 7) | See section 7 |

E5-mistral-7b is out of scope, because encoding 856,835 passages with a 7B model is not practical on this machine. This is a limit of the reproduction and is reported as such.

### 3.2 Libraries

| ID | Passages |
|---|---|
| `doctrine` | `wex` passages only (Cornell LII: encyclopedia entries, rules, UCC) |
| `cases` | `caselaw` passages only |
| `real` | `doctrine` plus `cases`. It leaves out the `mbe` passages, because those exist only because of the annotation |
| `full` | The whole published pool, as in the paper |

### 3.3 Readers

| ID | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `llama8` | `mlx-community/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct-4bit` | The paper reports this model, so it can be compared |
| `best-open` | The better of `Qwen3-8B-4bit` and `Phi-4-mini-instruct-4bit`, by closed-book accuracy on validation | A newer open model |
| `claude` | One Claude model through the CLI, on validation plus test only | Exploratory check of a much stronger reader. Not freely repeatable, and labelled so |

The local readers score the answer letters by their next-token probability and take the highest, as the paper does for Llama. Temperature does not apply.

Prompts are fixed on validation, then frozen.

### 3.4 Selection rules

All selection uses `validation` only:

- the best dense model: highest R@100 on `full`;
- k in {1, 3, 5} for context passages: highest reader accuracy, and ties go to the smaller k;
- the best real library for H1: highest reader accuracy among `doctrine`, `cases` and `real`.

Confirmatory runs use `train` plus `test` (1,071 questions) for every method that does not train on them. Methods that do train report on `test` only and are exploratory on `mbe`, because 117 questions are too few.

## 4. Statistics

- Comparisons are paired by question. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap over questions, with 10,000 resamples and seed 20261103.
- I report an exact McNemar test next to each accuracy difference.
- Holm correction runs across H1 to H10 (H10 comes from Amendment 1). Raw and corrected p-values are both reported. A verdict needs the corrected test as well as the rule below.

## 6. Confirmatory hypotheses

| ID | Claim | Measured on | Supported if |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Looking things up helps.** The best real library beats closed book | `llama8`, `mbe` | Difference at least 3 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H2 | **Outlines beat reporters.** `doctrine` beats `cases` | `llama8`, `mbe` | Difference at least 2 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H3 | **Better evidence still has room.** Gold beats the best real library | `llama8`, `mbe` | Difference at least 5 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H4 | **Rollouts help retrieval.** Local rollouts raise R@100 on `full`, for `bm25` and for the best dense model. Two tests | `mbe` | Each difference at least 3 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H5 | **The right state matters.** Retrieving within the state beats retrieving across all states | `llama8`, `housing`, best dense model, top 1 | Difference at least 3 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H6 | **Fine-tuning pays.** `ft-small` beats `e5-small` on R@10 | `housing`, held-out states | Difference at least 2 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H7 | **Bad law misleads.** With only the overruled passage, the model picks the superseded rule more often than closed book | `llama8`, `overruled` | Difference at least 10 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H8 | **A simple citator helps.** Dropping overruled decisions from retrieved passages lowers the superseded-rule rate | `llama8`, `overruled` | Difference at least 5 points and the interval excludes 0 |
| H9 | **Retrieval is blind to time.** A passage from the overruled decision outranks every passage from the overruling decision | Best dense model, `overruled` | The 95% Wilson lower bound on this share is above 30% |

A failed rule is a result. It is reported in the same place and at the same size as a success.

## 7. Fine-tuning

- Model: `e5-small`, multiple-negatives ranking loss, Apple GPU (MPS).
- `housing`: states are split by seed 20261103 into 60% train, 10% validation and 30% test. Question groups repeat across states, so we also report the results for question groups that do not appear in training.
- `mbe`: train on `train`, select on `validation`, report on `test`. Exploratory, because of the size.
- Learning rate, epochs and batch size are chosen on the validation split and frozen before the test evaluation.

## 8. The good-law test

### 8.1 Why

A lawyer must check that an authority is still good law. A retriever has no idea of time. The official table says which Supreme Court decisions the Court itself has overruled, so this test needs no AI to decide what was overruled.

### 8.2 Building the questions

1. Parse the table into (overruling decision, overruled decision) pairs. Keep pairs with available text for both opinions.
2. Claude Opus 5.5 reads both opinions. It writes one neutral question that names no case, plus four options: the current rule, the superseded rule and two plausible wrong answers. It records the sentences that support the first two.
3. Claude Sonnet 5.5, without seeing that reasoning, answers the question twice: given only the overruling opinion's passage, and given only the overruled opinion's passage. A question stays only if the first answer is the current rule and the second is the superseded rule.
4. Partial overrulings stay in the test only if the question targets the overruled part. The AI step records this.
5. Report how many pairs fail at each step. A random sample of 30 questions goes to `docs/review/` for the project author to check by hand. Until then, the questions are labelled "AI-written, not human-verified".

The table decides what was overruled. Only the wording of the questions is AI-made.

### 8.3 Conditions

| ID | The reader sees |
|---|---|
| `closed` | The question only |
| `old-only` | The passage from the overruled decision |
| `new-only` | The passage from the overruling decision |
| `both` | Both passages, with no dates |
| `both-dated` | Both passages, with case names and years |
| `retrieved` | Top k from the `scotus` corpus |
| `citator` | The same search, with overruled decisions dropped first |
| `note` | The same top k, with a note on any overruled decision: "Overruled by X (year)" |

Reported per condition: accuracy, superseded-rule rate, and other-wrong rate.

### 8.4 Exploratory audit

For each overruled decision, count passages in the `mbe` pool that cite it, using a citation regex. The pool's case law comes from 2019 to 2021, so citations to decisions overruled after that (Dobbs, Loper Bright and later) were good law when written and are not now. This count needs no AI.

## 9. Label audit (exploratory)

Recall@10 under 1% on `mbe` may partly reflect incomplete labels: one gold passage per question, in a pool of 856,835.

- Sample 100 questions from `validation` plus `test`, with seed 20261103. For each, pool the top 10 of `bm25` and the best dense model.
- Claude Opus 5.5 judges whether each passage states the rule that decides the question. Claude Sonnet 5.5 repeats this independently.
- Report the share of questions with at least one judged-relevant passage in the top 10, next to the gold recall, along with agreement between the judges.

These judgments are silver. They can suggest that the metric understates retrieval quality, but they cannot prove it.

## 10. Other exploratory analyses

These can suggest ideas and cannot support a claim:

- Accuracy by subject tag and by exam year, including whether questions from the 1970s fail more often.
- The `claude` reader on validation plus test.
- Context as concatenated top k against the paper's top-10 maximum-confidence method.
- One final run on `nextgen`, reported as a count of correct answers with the sample size, and never as a bar score.
- A partial check of the Magesh et al. hallucination figures on the public half of their data.

## 11. Error analysis

After the confirmatory runs, a seeded random sample of 100 wrong `llama8` answers with the best real library receives exactly one label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `retrieval` | The deciding authority is not in the context |
| `reading` | The deciding authority is present, and the reader still chose wrong |
| `out-of-library` | The rule lives somewhere that we cannot use, such as a Restatement |
| `law-changed` | The official answer rests on law that has since changed |
| `key-dispute` | The answer looks doubtful or depends on a contested view |
| `other` | None of these |

An AI model makes the first pass. Labels stay marked as such until the project author reviews them.

### Amendment 1, 2026-11-03: authority graph, citation extraction, research agent

I checked the plan against the official job text. Its focus areas include agentic AI, knowledge representation and dynamic ontologies, and it asks for exposure to agent frameworks, graphs and information extraction. The plan covered none of these directly, so this amendment adds them. No run has used any of this yet.

**A. Authority graph.** A small SQLite database built without AI:

- `cases(id, cite, name, date)` and `cites(src, dst)`, from Caselaw Access Project metadata;
- `overruled(old, new, year)`, from the official table.

`status(case, as_of_date)` returns whether a decision had been overruled on a given date, and by which decision. This is a dated fact store, so the same question can have different answers on different dates. It is the citator behind `citator` and `note` in section 8.3. We call it an authority graph and do not claim a full ontology or a graph database.

**B. Citation extraction (exploratory).** A regex extractor for U.S. Reports citations, scored against the `cites_to` metadata of the Caselaw Access Project on 500 randomly chosen opinions (seed 20261103). Report precision and recall. That metadata is a reference and may contain errors, so a sample of disagreements gets a manual look.

**C. Date test (exploratory).** Each `overruled` question is asked in two forms: "as of {year before the overruling}" and "as of today", with the `both-dated` context. The correct option differs between the two. Report the share of questions answered correctly in both forms.

**D. Research agent.** A hand-written loop of about 100 lines, with these tools:

- `search(query, library)`;
- `read(passage_id)`;
- `check_status(citation, as_of_date)`.

The budget is 5 tool calls per question. Tool calls use a fixed JSON format. The same tools are also served over MCP, so that an outside agent can use them. Every run logs a full trace.

- Reader: `best-open`. As an exploratory comparison, a Claude model runs the same tools over MCP through the CLI.
- Questions: all of `overruled`, and `mbe` validation plus test.
- Reported: accuracy, superseded-rule rate, tool calls per question, the share of malformed calls, runs that hit the budget, and runs that ignored a `check_status` result.

New hypothesis, added to the Holm family (now H1 to H10):

| ID | Claim | Measured on | Supported if |
|---|---|---|---|
| H10 | **An agent with a citator is safer than one-shot retrieval.** The agent has a lower superseded-rule rate than `retrieved` with the same reader | `best-open`, `overruled` | Difference at least 5 points and the interval excludes 0 |

If `best-open` cannot produce valid tool calls on validation questions in at least 80% of its turns, H10 is recorded as "not testable with this model". That is a finding about the small model and it is not a reason to change the reader.

### Amendment 2, 2026-11-03: what this machine can run

Speeds were measured on samples of up to about 6 minutes, so long runs may be slower. Only BM25 has run on real data so far. No reader has run.

| Model | `mbe` passages/s | `mbe` hours | `housing` passages/s | `housing` hours |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `e5-small` | 193.8 | 1.2 | 64.1 | 7.9 |

| `e5-large` | 10.4 | 22.4 | 4.2 | 121 |

Decisions:

- **Runs.** `e5-small` runs on both datasets, and `e5-base` on `mbe`. `e5-large` runs on `mbe` after the confirmatory runs, if the machine is free.
- **R2c.** `e5-base` runs on `housing` within 10 randomly chosen states (seed 20261103), which should take about 8 hours. Its verdict is marked "on sampled states", and the same states are reported for `bm25` and `e5-small` next to it.
- **R2d.** `e5-large` on `housing` is not attempted, because it would take about 121 hours. This is reported as a limit.
- **H5 and H6** use `e5-small`, since it is the only dense model with all states encoded.
- **Housing reader sample.** Reader runs on `housing` use 2,000 questions, stratified by state, drawn with seed 20261103 before any reader runs. R3 and H5 are measured on this sample. The R3 verdict says so.
- **R1 and R2 use all questions.** The reproduction targets score all 1,195 `mbe` questions and all 6,853 `housing` questions to match the paper, so BM25 has already touched `train` and `test`. No selection used those scores. Selection stays on `validation`, as section 3.4 says.
- **BM25 tokenizer.** The `plain` tokenizer stays the verdict and the retriever used downstream. On `housing` the `porter` tokenizer lands within 0.2 points of the published values, so tokenization is the likely cause of the R2a gap. It stays a labelled diagnostic.
- **Gold recall and libraries.** The data audit found that all 1,195 gold passages have source `mbe`, and only one gold text also appears in another source. Gold recall therefore cannot measure `doctrine`, `cases` or `real`. Those libraries are compared by reader accuracy only (H1, H2), and the label audit in section 9 gives the other view.
- **Rank files.** Only the top 100 of each ranking stays in git. The top 1000 goes to the data folder. All metrics are computed before this cut and are stored per query in the run file. The four rank files already committed stay as they are.

### Amendment 3, 2026-11-03: the good-law data

No reader has seen any good-law question. I read the five questions of the generator's pilot, to check that the method works. Those five stay in the test, and this is disclosed here.

- **Counts.** Section 2 says 271 overruled decisions, which came from counting rows and semicolons. Some cells join names with commas alone. The parsed table has 237 rows, 293 pairs and 283 distinct overruled decisions. Text for both decisions exists for 291 pairs.
- **The current rule.** "Current rule" means the rule of the overruling decision. A question leaves the test if the table shows that the overruling decision was itself overruled later. The count is reported. A later statute could also change the rule. The table cannot show that, so the hand-review sample checks it and the limit is stated.
- **Opinion types.** The `scotus` corpus keeps majority, concurring and dissenting opinions, and each passage records its type. Dissents are a real failure mode: a dissent can read like law. Report the share of each type in retrieved passages. The `citator` condition drops overruled decisions only.
- **Citation scoring.** The extractor was scored first against CAP's link paths (precision 0.971, recall 0.988). Reading the disagreements showed that those paths do not always give the first page, so the reference changed to CAP's printed citations (precision 0.986, recall 1.000). Both results stay in the record, and the second comes from a choice made after seeing the first. A post hoc sample of opinions of at least 300 words gave precision 0.991 and recall 0.998.
- **Pool audit.** An independent recount with plain regular expressions gave almost the same numbers as the project's audit: Chevron 210, Casey 72, Roe 35 against 36, Humphrey's Executor 8, Colorado Republican 3. Citing a decision does not mean relying on it, so these counts measure exposure and not error.

### Amendment 4, 2026-11-03: gaps in selection and in fine-tuning

No reader has run on `train` or `test`. Smoke runs used at most 10 validation questions, to check the plumbing and measure speed. Prompts changed during those runs because Qwen3 did not answer with a letter. The prompt version is `v3`, and it is now frozen.

- **Retriever for H1 to H3.** The reader uses the best dense model from section 3.4. BM25 runs as a comparison, and it sets no verdict.
- **Order of selection.** First the best dense model, by R@100 on validation. Then k, on the `real` library. Then the library, at that k.
- **Ties.** A tie between dense models goes to the smaller model. A tie between libraries goes to the first of `doctrine`, `cases`, `real`. A tie between open readers goes to Qwen3.
- **Holm family.** H4 has two tests, so the family has 11 tests.
- **Tests for H4 and H6.** Both compare hits at the stated k with an exact McNemar test next to the bootstrap interval.
- **Answer tokens.** On `housing`, the reader scores "Yes" and "No", where the paper says Y or N. This is a protocol difference, and the R3 verdict says so.
- **Fine-tuning on `mbe` is dropped.** With 117 test questions it could not support a claim, and the gold passages exist only because of the annotation. H6 on `housing` stays.
- **Context length.** The current limit of 2,000 tokens for context was an untuned choice. It may change once, after measuring speed on an idle machine on validation questions, and before any confirmatory run. The change will be a dated line here.

### Amendment 5, 2026-11-04: H9 test, workers and the code limit

No reader has run on `train`, `test` or the good-law questions.

- **H9 p-value.** Section 6 names no test for H9. For the Holm correction, H9 uses an exact one-sided binomial test against 30%. Its verdict rule does not change.
- **Workers.** Implementation and review may also use Claude Sonnet, Claude Haiku and two OpenAI Codex models (`gpt-6.1-terra`, `gpt-5.6-terra`). The AI roles fixed in sections 8.2 and 9 do not change: Opus 5.5 writes or judges first, and Sonnet 5.5 checks. Only the conductor's review counts as acceptance.

### Amendment 6, 2026-11-07: the H6 grid

The fine-tuned model has not run on any test state. Only one smoke run has trained it, on 40 pairs.

- **Grid.** Three training runs on the training states, with learning rates 1e-5, 2e-5 and 5e-5. Each run has 1 epoch, batch size 16 and temperature 0.01, and keeps 20,000 statutes per validation state.
- **Choice.** The run with the highest validation R@10 after its last epoch goes to the test. A tie goes to the smaller learning rate. That run is evaluated once on the test states with `eval --split test`.
- **Why so small.** Each run takes about an hour on this machine. More epochs or batch sizes would cost days and the gain is uncertain. The grid and its results are reported in full.

### Amendment 7, 2026-11-07: a batching defect in the H6 grid

No fine-tuned model has run on a test state.

- **What happened.** The three runs of Amendment 6 all lowered validation R@10. The zero-shot model scored 50.75. After one epoch the scores were 19.10 at 1e-5 (run `b628f684d20678d0`), 20.87 at 2e-5 (`58ffe701f96ad482`) and 36.43 at 5e-5 (`fdc6a2962798a444`).
- **Cause found.** The 4,266 training questions use only 226 distinct question texts, because housing asks the same question in many states. The batching step checked question ids, not question text. So 297 of the 534 batches held the same question text twice, with a different state's statute as its answer. Each of those statutes then counted as a wrong answer for the other copy. The docstring already said that no question may repeat in a batch, so the code did not do what the design said.
- **Fix.** A batch now holds each question text and each statute text at most once. The number of batches stays 534.
- **What happens next.** The same grid runs again with `--rerun 1`, with the same selection rule from Amendment 6. The three runs above stay in the record and are reported as a failed attempt. If the new runs also fall below the zero-shot model on validation, the chosen run still goes to the test once, as Amendment 6 says.

### Amendment 8, 2026-11-07: the corrected grid and the test evaluation

No fine-tuned model has run on a test state.

- **Corrected grid.** After the fix of Amendment 7, validation R@10 was 21.56 at 1e-5 (`def9a6b167863e14`), 20.87 at 2e-5 (`428a001d43e56e9c`) and 36.83 at 5e-5 (`a37ef8eb21b1b248`). All three are below the zero-shot 50.75.
- **Diagnostic, post hoc.** A scratch check on 3 training states and the 5 validation states, with 5,000 random statutes per state, gave these R@10 values. Zero-shot: 61.46 on training states and 68.35 on validation states. At 5e-5: 71.54 and 55.53. At 1e-5: 42.32 and 32.20. Fitting the training states while losing the new ones looks like overfitting. That does not explain why 1e-5 also lost recall on its own training states. The cause is not known.
- **Second opinion.** An OpenAI Codex model (`gpt-6.1-sol`) read the code. It found one real defect: training seeded NumPy but not PyTorch, so dropout was not seeded, and the six runs cannot be repeated exactly. Training now seeds PyTorch too. The checkpoints are kept, and nothing is retrained because of this. Its claim that R@10 should be per-statute recall was rejected: section 5 defines recall on `housing` as at least one gold statute found. Its other points are design limits: training truncates at 256 tokens and evaluation at 512, and in-batch negatives are other questions' gold statutes.
- **Test.** As Amendments 6 and 7 require, `a37ef8eb21b1b248` is evaluated once on the test states. Experiments about the cause run after that, and they are labelled exploratory.

### Amendment 9, 2026-11-08: after the H6 test

This amendment changes no rule and no verdict. It records two facts about the H6 test run (`bd578ac4833d144c`).

- **Result.** On the 1,854 questions of the 14 test states, R@10 was 30.58 for `a37ef8eb21b1b248` and 31.18 for zero-shot `e5-small`. H6 is not supported. All 11 tests in the Holm family have now run.
- **No unseen question groups.** Every question group in the test states also appears in the training states. So the exploratory subset of unseen groups is empty. The run file stores 0 for it, which is wrong, because an empty subset has no rate. The summary now reports it as missing, and later evaluations store it as missing.
